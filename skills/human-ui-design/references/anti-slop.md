# AI design tells: challenge the pattern

| Tell | Problem | Alternative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tracked uppercase eyebrow before every heading | Synthetic editorial repetition | Let type scale and space establish hierarchy; label only meaningful things |
| Decorative “01 / 02 / 03” chapter headings | Faux structure | Use numbering only for an actual sequence |
| Tiny grey meta strings and labels | Difficult to read, mostly decorative | Promote useful information; delete empty metadata |
| Beige, sage, terracotta by default | Generic warm-premium recipe | Derive colour from brand, subject and imagery |
| Oversized high-contrast serif everywhere | Generic luxury/editorial cue | Choose typography for voice and use, including strong sans faces |
| Three equal cards in every section | Templates every idea | Tell stories with evidence, imagery, contrast and variation |
| Purple gradients, glowing blobs, glass pills | Stock AI-SaaS veneer | Let real product screenshots and interactions lead |
| Rounded pills for all actions and stats | Shapes replace hierarchy | Reserve shapes for actual affordances and states |
| Decorative arrow after every CTA | Repetitive noise | Use verbs and clear link/button styling |
| Reveal animation on everything | Slows reading, distracts | Motion only when it helps orientation or emotion |
| Generated photos of people: golden-hour glow, everyone smiling at each other, flawless skin, tidy props, centred posed groups | Read as AI instantly, even when labelled; undermines every true claim beside them | Real photography first; otherwise follow “When there are no real photographs” in [art-direction.md](art-direction.md) |
| “Empowering communities” abstraction | Could describe any company | Say what the organisation actually does |
| Every section built the same way (big headline one side, paragraph the other, full-width colour band) | The page becomes one pattern repeated; the story never changes pace | Give each section the layout its content needs; break the pattern at least once with a full-bleed image, a sequence, a quote, or real data |
| Headlines that are all short slogans ending in a full stop (“Make good last.” “Let's talk.”) | A recognisable AI copy cadence; each line sounds like the last | Mix forms: a plain statement of fact, a question, a name, a number, a line in someone's own words |
| Small light-grey body text, especially on coloured bands | Fails contrast and reads as decoration, not information | Body text at 16px or more in a dark ink; measure contrast on the actual background |

## Second-order tells

Ban the list above and agents reach for the next set of defaults: the “distinctive” moves that award sites made popular. They read as AI just as quickly once they appear together or without a reason. Treat each as a warning that needs a content reason, not as a style choice.

| Tell | Why it reads as generated | When it is right |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inter, Geist, Space Grotesk, Satoshi or Instrument Serif as the “safe distinctive” pick | The default answer when told to avoid the default | The brand already uses it, or its letterforms suit the voice better than the alternatives you tried |
| Monospace micro-labels and coordinates (`[01]`, `EST. 2024`, `LAT −26.1`) | The second-generation eyebrow | Real technical data, code or measurements |
| Bento grid of mismatched tiles | The second-generation three-card grid | A real dashboard or a product with genuinely different features to show side by side |
| Marquee or ticker of repeated words | Movement with nothing to say | Live or changing content: scores, departures, a real feed |
| Grain, noise or film overlay | Texture standing in for imagery | The brand's own print or film material |
| Giant outlined or background words, oversized decorative numbers | Scale used as ornament | A figure that is the message and is explained beside it |
| Full-width footer wordmark | The sign-off every template now has | The name is the brand's main asset and nothing more useful belongs there |
| Rotated sticker badges, ✦ sparkles, pulsing “available” dots | Charm applied to anything | A real status, or a sticker the brand actually prints |
| Lime or acid accent on off-black | The “brutalist” preset | The brand's real colours |
| Hairline grid lines, crosshair corners, frame marks everywhere | Swiss-grid cosplay | A layout whose grid carries information, such as a timetable or specification |
| Pinned scroll chapters, word-by-word reveals, split-letter headline animation | Motion that slows reading to look crafted | One moment where sequence is the story, with a reduced-motion fallback |
| Custom cursors, magnetic buttons | Interaction as decoration | A portfolio or toy whose subject is interaction |
| Fake UI chrome: browser frames, terminals, floating cards with invented metrics | Proof-shaped decoration | Real screenshots of the real product |
| Floating glass navigation pill | The current template nav | Rarely; a plain bar is usually better |

The test: would this be here if nobody were going to screenshot the page? If two or more of these appear without a content reason, rework the composition rather than swapping one for another.

## Audit

- Could a different company replace the logo without affecting the design? If so, the concept is too generic.
- Are motifs repeated because they help comprehension or because they make screenshots look designed?
- Is the type visually impressive **and** readable?
- Does every section add a new fact, feeling, proof, or action?
- Are images meaningful and truthful? Would a stranger guess a photo was generated?
- Shrink the full-page screenshot to a thumbnail: do the sections look like copies of one another?
- Read every headline in order as a list: do they share one cadence?
- Which second-order tells are on the page, and what content reason does each have?
- Does the palette have a brand rationale?
- Can users understand and complete the main action?
- Is mobile deliberately composed?
- Are imagery licenses, facts, and external forms verifiable?

When a user says “AI slop,” inspect the **visual grammar**. If the issue is conceptual, revisit art direction and storytelling, not just borders and shadows.
