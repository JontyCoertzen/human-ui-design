# AI design tells: challenge the pattern

| Tell | Problem | Alternative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tracked uppercase eyebrow before every heading | Synthetic editorial repetition | Let type scale and space establish hierarchy; label only meaningful things |
| Decorative “01 / 02 / 03” chapter headings | Faux structure | Use numbering only for an actual sequence |
| Tiny grey meta strings and labels | Difficult to read, mostly decorative | Promote useful information; delete empty metadata |
| Beige, sage, terracotta by default | Generic warm-premium recipe | Derive colour from brand, subject and imagery |
| Oversized high-contrast serif everywhere | Generic luxury/editorial cue | Choose typography for voice and use, including strong sans faces |
| Three equal cards in every section | Templates every idea | Tell stories with evidence, imagery, contrast and variation |
| Purple gradients, glowing blobs, glass pills | Stock AI-SaaS veneer | Let real product screenshots and interactions lead |
| Rounded pills for all actions and stats | Shapes replace hierarchy | Reserve shapes for actual affordances and states |
| Decorative arrow after every CTA | Repetitive noise | Use verbs and clear link/button styling |
| Reveal animation on everything | Slows reading, distracts | Motion only when it helps orientation or emotion |
| Perfectly posed charity “beneficiaries” | Weak credibility, risk of misrepresentation | Use genuine documented people or clearly labelled illustrative imagery |
| “Empowering communities” abstraction | Could describe any company | Say what the organisation actually does |
| Identical section padding/widths | Artificial visual rhythm | Vary scale/density to serve the narrative |

## Audit

- Could a different company replace the logo without affecting the design? If so, the concept is too generic.
- Are motifs repeated because they help comprehension or because they make screenshots look designed?
- Is the type visually impressive **and** readable?
- Does every section add a new fact, feeling, proof, or action?
- Are images meaningful and truthful?
- Does the palette have a brand rationale?
- Can users understand and complete the main action?
- Is mobile deliberately composed?
- Are imagery licenses, facts, and external forms verifiable?

When a user says “AI slop,” inspect the **visual grammar**. If the issue is conceptual, revisit art direction and storytelling, not just borders and shadows.
