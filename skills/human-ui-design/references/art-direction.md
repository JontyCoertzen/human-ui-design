# Art direction: find the real idea

## Five questions before designing

1. **Audience:** Who is this for and what do they already know?
2. **Five-second comprehension:** What must be understood before scrolling?
3. **Feeling:** What emotion, more specific than “premium,” is appropriate?
4. **Proof:** Which real person, place, product, result, or story earns trust?
5. **Action:** What should the visitor do next and how few steps can it take?

## Compact creative brief

Fill this in before writing components:

> **Premise:** one sentence unique to this project.
>
> **Voice:** three adjectives and one example of natural copy.
>
> **Type:** display/body personality, font choice, sizing, line length, and rationale.
>
> **Colour:** palette, contrast, and a brand-driven reason.
>
> **Imagery:** subjects, framing, lighting, source/rights, authenticity.
>
> **Composition:** hero relationship, section cadence, whitespace, image density.
>
> **Motion:** purpose, limits, reduced-motion fallback.
>
> **Conversion:** primary action, placement, trust details.
>
> **Avoid:** at least three project-specific anti-references.

## Ownable direction

- Find a true phrase, history, ritual, place, object, or tension particular to the brand.
- Derive style from content and constraints, not from an archetype preset.
- Explain what works in references and adapt the underlying principle; do not clone.
- Make typography perform structural work. Headlines must communicate, not just decorate.
- Prefer client-provided imagery. Use real, credible environments and documentary framing when appropriate.
- Change composition when the story changes; avoid identical centred headings and three-card blocks throughout.
- Make mobile an intentional composition, not merely a narrow desktop view.

## Copy and proof

- Write like a real person with a clear point of view.
- Prefer concrete nouns and verbs. Remove unearned claims such as “empowering change”.
- Use one memorable headline plus a plain explanatory sentence.
- Eliminate repeated pitch paragraphs that add nothing.
- Put trust details near conversion: payment processing, donation allocation, eligibility, refunds, privacy, and legal identity as relevant.
- Never invent people, figures, testimonials, institutional affiliations, or product interfaces.
- Clearly distinguish user-provided real photography, licensed stock, and generated concept imagery.

## New site versus approved site

**New:** Explore visual distinction. A civic charity, cultural venue, esports platform, wellness clinic, and SaaS product should not automatically share an aesthetic.

**Existing:** Respect approved typography, palette, spacing, image treatment, navigation, and component conventions. Extend them; do not reinterpret the identity unless asked.
